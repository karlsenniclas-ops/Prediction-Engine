      Matching evidence

      Settlement & execution Review required

      Price agreement does not prove identical settlement. Check observation windows, sources, cancellations and outcome orientation. Snapshots are not simultaneous fills; event-end status and latency can invalidate an edge.

      Method Ask-based · depth-aware · no double-counted spread

      Buying complementary outcomes pays $1 only when the contracts cover every settlement state. We walk both ask books to the same quantity, subtract venue fees, and limit size to the thinner book. Shared or mirrored liquidity is not added across routes.

      The single market charts plot the Yes ask against $1 minus the No ask over the life of the bet. The fill is the net edge of buying both: green when Yes ask + No ask + fees stays below $1, red when it does not. Kalshi's asks come from its hourly or daily candles. Polymarket has no public ask history, so its path is the volume-weighted price takers paid for each token per hour, as Saguillo et al. do with trades, and the API only serves the latest 11,000 trades. A price is carried forward until the next one, for at most a day (two days for daily candles); after that the line breaks and there is no fill.

      The cross-market price history applies the same test to one route at a time, following Cheng et al.: the Yes ask on one venue plus the No ask on the other. Arbitrage at size uses the live books instead of history.

      Kelly sizing needs a defensible probability and loss model for execution failure, so the engine does not invent a Kelly stake. Mint-and-sell (bids above $1) and combinatorial strategies are outside this two-leg scanner. Fees follow the Polymarket fee docs and the Kalshi fee schedule.

      Inspiration & references

      This project started from two papers on arbitrage in prediction markets. Saguillo et al. measured how often Yes and No prices on Polymarket drift away from $1, and Gebele et al. showed that much of that edge disappears once you walk the real order book instead of trusting the top quote. I wanted to see whether the same holds for one bet listed on both Kalshi and Polymarket. The single market and cross-market conditions follow Cheng et al.

      1. Saguillo, O., Ghafouri, V., Kiffer, L. & Suarez-Tangil, G. (2025). Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets. arXiv:2508.03474. arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474
      2. Gebele, J., Mutzel, T. & Matthes, F. (2026). Executable Arbitrage and Market Efficiency in Prediction Markets. arXiv:2608.00666. arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666
      3. Cheng, G., Yang, J. & Zou, H. (2026). Arbitrage Analysis in Polymarket NBA Markets. arXiv:2605.00864. arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864
